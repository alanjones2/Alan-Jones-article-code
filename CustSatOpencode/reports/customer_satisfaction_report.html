Methodology and limitations

  • Source: data/shoes.md; all six listed products and 24 reviews are included. No external evidence was added. Four reviews are supplied for each product.
  • Calculations: mean = sum of ratings ÷ review count; positive share = count rated 4 or 5 ÷ count; low share = count rated 1 or 2 ÷ count. Three-star reviews are neutral. Portfolio mean weights each review equally. Ranks use unrounded means and competition ranking (1, 2, 2, 4…). Percentages and means are rounded only for display.
  • Qualitative coding: concerns and strengths were assigned manually to each comment and are included in the evidence appendix and exported data. A review can contain both; a concern in a 4-star review does not make that review low-rated. Themes describe reported experiences, not established root causes. There was no independent second coder.
  • Uncertainty: the selection method, review dates, sales volumes, purchaser verification and customer characteristics are unknown. Selection bias and differing use conditions may affect comparisons. One review is 25% of a product sample; a one-star change in one review changes that product’s mean by 0.25. The 0.25-point lead over the joint runners-up should not be treated as statistically established superiority.
  • Scope: no trend, financial impact, defect prevalence or causal inference can be established from these comments alone. Recommendations identify what to investigate; product testing and broader feedback are needed to substantiate causes and measure improvements.
